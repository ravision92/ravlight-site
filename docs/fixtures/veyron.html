Veyron — pixel bar

Forty RGB pixels in two rows and six white segments, in a one-metre bar built for the stage. Patch it pixel by pixel or as a single colour, let 32 built-in macros carry your console's colours across both rows, and run it over Ethernet, WiFi or plain DMX. stable

Quick start

  1. Power. Plug the first bar in with the PowerCON – Schuko cable and link the next ones from POWER OUT. One line takes 10–12 bars; protect it with a 16 A type C breaker and an RCD.
  2. Data. Each bar on an Ethernet switch (Art-Net or sACN), or a 3-pin XLR DMX chain with a 120 Ω terminator on the last bar. Every bar reads one universe.
  3. Connect. With no network the bar opens its own WiFi: Veyron-RVxxxx, password 123456789, then http://192.168.4.1. On a network: http://ravRVxxxx.local. RVxxxx is the SN on the label.
  4. Patch. In DMX pick source and universe; in Veyron pick personality and address. Several bars on one universe: address = 1 + (n − 1) × channels.
  5. Reset. Hold RESET for 5 seconds to go back to factory settings.

Wiring

Whatever the route, each bar receives one DMX universe. Ethernet DMX WiFi Power

Ethernet network with a switch

Recommended. Each bar to a switch port; the console sends Art-Net or sACN. Configuration and updates remotely.

WiFi through a router

The bar joins the venue WiFi (Network section). Handy where cable cannot reach; for shows prefer Ethernet.

The bar's access point

With no network the bar opens its own WiFi. Use it for the first setup, from a phone or laptop.

DMX cable chain

3-pin XLR from DMX IN to DMX OUT, 120 Ω terminator on the last bar. Every bar reads the same universe.

Bar as a DMX node

A networked bar repeats its universe on DMX OUT (DMX Node Output) and drives other bars or DMX fixtures over cable.

Power linking

PowerCON from IN to OUT, 10–12 bars per line. Line protected by a 16 A type C breaker and a 30 mA RCD.

Geometry and orientation

Seen from the front, pixel 1 is top left and the PowerCON input is on the right. The 40 pixels form one serpentine chain: 1 → 20 along the top row, 21 → 40 back along the bottom row, so pixel 40 sits below pixel 1. The six white segments W1–W6 are numbered from the left; each one lights the same section on all three white strips.

A bar hung upside down or mirrored against its neighbour is fixed in the fixture settings with Flip X (pixel 1 on the right) and Flip Y (rows swapped). The flip only changes the LED output: DMX layout, console profiles and pixel maps stay valid.

Each personality has three blocks, RGB, White and Function, with their own start addresses. Leave them consecutive and you get the footprint above, which is also what the console profiles expect. Full channel tables are in the wiki and the manual.

Macros

One macro set serves Pixel Macro (P2), Zone Macro (P4, P6) and RGB Macro (P8). Most macros keep the colour you patched and move a brightness mask over it, pixel by pixel or zone by zone; a few bring their own colour. They are drawn for the bar's real two-row geometry, so a chase reads as one sweep across both rows.

Shutter and dimmers

RGB and whites each have their own shutter and master dimmer (P8 has one Master Intensity). The shutter byte picks function and speed together; Open sits at both ends, so a channel at 0 or 255 leaves the light on.

Without a console

  • Signal loss: hold, black, play a show or run an effect when DMX stops; DMX takes back over when it returns.
  • Shows: play uncompressed FSEQ files from xLights and similar, or record what the bar receives, up to 5 minutes.
  • Built-in effects: the whole macro set, colour, speed and shutter from the web interface.
  • Status on the bar: amber comets while connecting, magenta when the access point is up, green when connected, blue progress during an update. Highlight flashes the bar for 6 s to find it in a rig.
